Amortization Truths

Why Regular EMIs Keep You Trapped in Debt

Standard banking schedules front-load interest payments, ensuring the bank profits first before you build meaningful home equity.

🪤

The Front-Loaded Trap

In the first 5–8 years of a home loan, up to 70–80% of every monthly EMI payment goes purely towards interest, hardly reducing the actual borrowed principal.

⚖️

Tenure vs. EMI Choice

Choose between slashing years off your loan to achieve lifelong debt freedom or reducing your monthly EMI burden to enhance immediate monthly liquidity.

📈

The Step-Up Edge

Incrementing your extra prepayment by just 5–10% each year aligns with your annual salary increments and knocks decade-long mortgages down to under 12 years.

Understanding Your Prepayment Impact

Every extra rupee paid against your loan principal changes future interest compounding. Compare how different prepayment methods perform.

Strategy 01

Tenure Reduction (Recommended)

Keep your monthly EMI constant while extra prepayments knock off months from the end of the loan. This minimizes total interest paid across the loan tenure.

Strategy 02

EMI Reduction Mode

Maintain the original maturity date but lower your mandatory monthly EMI. Ideal for borrowers anticipating lifestyle expenses, career transitions, or family commitments.

Strategy 03

1 Extra EMI per Year

A simple discipline of paying 13 EMIs in 12 months using your annual bonus slashes over 3.5 years off a 20-year home loan without altering your monthly budget.

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about home loan prepayments, RBI guidelines, tenure reductions, and step-ups.

Is it better to reduce loan tenure or reduce EMI when prepaying? ▼
Reducing loan tenure yields significantly higher interest savings because it shortens the compound interest accumulation period. When you keep your EMI constant and reduce tenure, each subsequent EMI repays a larger proportion of principal. Reducing EMI lowers your monthly cash outflow but saves considerably less total interest over the lifetime of the mortgage.
Should I prepay my home loan or invest the surplus in an equity SIP? ▼
Prepaying a home loan gives a guaranteed, risk-free post-tax return equal to your loan interest rate (typically 8.5%–9.5% p.a.). Equity SIPs have historically delivered 12%–14% annualized returns over 10+ year horizons but carry market volatility. A balanced strategy involves allocating 50% of surplus cash to loan prepayments for guaranteed interest savings and 50% to equity SIPs for long-term wealth creation.
Are there prepayment penalties on home loans in India? ▼
As per Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulations, banks and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) are strictly prohibited from charging any prepayment or foreclosure penalties on floating-rate home loans availed by individual borrowers. However, fixed-rate home loans or loans taken in the name of a business entity / non-individual borrower may attract a 2% to 4% prepayment penalty.
How does a Step-Up prepayment accelerate loan closure? ▼
As your salary or business revenues grow over time, increasing your monthly prepayment surplus by 5% to 10% each year (Step-Up) exponentially compounds your debt repayment velocity. For instance, stepping up a ₹5,000 monthly prepayment by 10% annually on a ₹50 Lakh loan reduces a 20-year tenure down to just 11.6 years, saving over ₹27 Lakhs in interest.
